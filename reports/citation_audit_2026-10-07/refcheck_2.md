## azaria2023internal

**METADATA: OK.** Title, the 2 authors, Findings of EMNLP 2023, pages 967--976, DOI 10.18653/v1/2023.findings-emnlp.68 and arXiv 2304.13734 all match.
- https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.68.bib
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13734

**CLAIMS**
1. "detected lies after the fact with probes on internal representations [burns, azaria, marks]": **SUPPORTED**, with one nuance. SAPLMA is "a classifier that receives as input the activation values of the hidden layers of an LLM ... determines for each statement generated by the LLM if it is true or false" (Sec. 1). What it detects is false statements, which the paper calls "lying"; it does not test instructed lies. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13734
2. "new true/false statements [AM/GoT; azaria, marks]": **SUPPORTED**. The paper builds a True-False dataset of 6 topics (Cities, Inventions, Chemical Elements, Animals, Companies, Scientific Facts) and says it "is available at azariaa.com".
3. "Truth directions in representations [burns, azaria, marks] underlie probes that detect lies after the fact": **PARTIAL**. Azaria and Mitchell do not find a direction. SAPLMA is "a simple and relatively shallow feedforward neural network" with "three hidden layers ... (256, 128, 64)", which is a nonlinear probe. Suggested fix: "Truth signals in representations, linear directions \citep{burns2023ccs,marks2024geometry} or nonlinear probes \citep{azaria2023internal}, underlie detectors that flag false statements after the fact".
4. "Injected: ... 400 true/false statements from \citet{azaria2023internal} topics used nowhere else": **SUPPORTED**, because the dataset is organised by topic as in item 2. The 400-statement count belongs to the citing paper and was not checked. Style: "from Azaria and Mitchell (2023) topics" reads awkwardly. Better: "from topics of the true/false dataset of \citet{azaria2023internal}".
5. "AM/GoT: ... 1,100 new true/false statements from \citet{azaria2023internal} and \citet{marks2024geometry}": **SUPPORTED**, since the dataset exists. The count is internal to the citing paper.

## lee2019snip

**METADATA: OK.** Title, 3 authors and arXiv 1810.02340 match. The arXiv comment says "ICLR 2019", and the OpenReview ICLR 2019 forum is B1VZqjAcYX.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02340
- https://openreview.net/forum?id=B1VZqjAcYX

**CLAIMS**
1. "Pruning scores from forward passes \citep{sun2023wanda,lee2019snip,frankle2019lottery}": **UNSUPPORTED for SNIP**. SNIP's saliency is the normalised magnitude of the derivative of the loss with respect to the connection masks, s_j = |g_j| / sum_k |g_k|, which "can be computed efficiently in one forward-backward pass using automatic differentiation" (Sec. 3, Eq. 5-6). It is gradient-based and is computed at initialisation. https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02340
   - Side note on frankle2019lottery, which is not in my batch but sits in the same clause: the lottery ticket paper prunes "the smallest-magnitude weights" (iterative magnitude pruning, arXiv 1803.03635). That score uses no forward passes either.
   - Suggested fix: "Pruning importance scores, from weight magnitude \citep{frankle2019lottery}, gradients \citep{lee2019snip} or forward-pass activations \citep{sun2023wanda}, became a safety probe in \citet{wei2024brittleness}, ...". Alternatively, keep "from forward passes" and cite only \citep{sun2023wanda}.

## schapiro2025creativityneuro

**METADATA: OK.** Title, 4 authors (Schapiro, Park, Sosa, Varshney), arXiv 2607.01433 (v1, 2026-07-01) and year 2026 match. The arXiv comment reads "Accepted at ICML 2026 Workshop on Creativity & Generative AI", which matches the booktitle. The key says 2025, which is cosmetic.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01433

**CLAIMS**
1. "... and CreativityNeuro changes a behaviour with a similar contrast": **SUPPORTED**. Sec. 3 uses "the same Wanda-style (Sun et al., 2023) parameter importance scoring" on creative versus non-creative prompts and keeps "the top ρ percent of weights ranked by creative importance that do not also appear in the top ρ percent for non-creative prompts".
2. "CreativityNeuro rescales weights selected by a creative-versus-conventional contrast": **SUPPORTED**. Algorithm 1 sets W' = W ⊙ (1 + α·M) on the set C \ N. The prompt sets contrast creative prompts with non-creative ones (e.g. "Surprise me." vs "Be precise.", and "Create a typical story beginning ...", Table 1).
3. Appendix: "selection rule (graded contrast ... against set difference, top-ρ by |W| n^F minus top-ρ by |W| n^T)": **SUPPORTED**, matching Alg. 1 lines 7-9.
   "activation statistic (per-prompt mean of the channel norms against one pooled-token norm, the root mean square of the per-prompt norms, as in Wanda)": **PARTIAL/UNSUPPORTED** as a description of CreativityNeuro.
   - CN's Algorithm 1 writes S = Σ_b Σ_t |W_ij|·‖x_j^(b,t)‖₂, a sum over all prompts and token positions. Read literally, that is an L1 pooling over tokens, not the RMS of per-prompt norms.
   - CN states it uses "the same ... scoring as Christ et al. (2025)" (MathNeuro, arXiv 2410.16930, ACL 2025 Main per its arXiv comment).
   - MathNeuro defines S = Σ_k |W_ij|·‖X^k_j‖₂, a sum over samples of per-sample token-L2 norms. Its released code (github.com/bryanchrist/MathNeuro, MathNeuro.py) computes `activations.norm(p=2, dim=1)` per prompt and adds up the per-prompt scores. That is the per-prompt statistic the citing paper attributes to its own rule.
   - So the CN paper does not support "RMS of per-prompt norms, as in Wanda" as CN's statistic. The 2×2 column labelled "set difference on pooled-token norms (CreativityNeuro's rule)" may be mislabelled. The paper reports the statistic is worth only −1.1 points, so the main conclusion probably stands.
   - Suggested fix: "... and the activation statistic (per-prompt mean of channel norms against Wanda's single pooled-token norm). CreativityNeuro's Algorithm 1 sums over prompts and tokens, and the MathNeuro code it follows \citep{christ2025mathneuro} sums per-prompt norms, so we run its set-difference rule with both statistics."
   - Relabel the cell, for example "set difference, pooled-token (Wanda) norms". The same wording appears in the paper's "Against CreativityNeuro's rule" paragraph ("top-ρ ... on pooled-token norms") and in the Table cn2x2 caption.
   - Also consider citing MathNeuro: CN calls its own contribution "a framework for extending MathNeuro" (Sec. 3), so the set-difference-and-scale rule originates there.
   - Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01433, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16930, https://github.com/bryanchrist/MathNeuro

- Add: volume = 37, pages = {139179--139212}, doi = {10.52202/079017-4417}, publisher Curran Associates.

**CLAIMS**
1. "its effect depends on the prompts that built the vector and on the setting in which it is applied [tan, braun, nguyen]": **SUPPORTED** for Tan. Sec. 6 says "the effectiveness of a steering vector is mostly a property of the dataset used to extract [it]". Vectors are extracted in one prompt setting and applied in another, and "SVs generalise better over some shifts than others".
2. "activation addition, whose vectors are sensitive to the prompts that build them [tan, braun]": **SUPPORTED**. Tan extracts SVs under different system/user-prompt injections, finds transfer depends on how similarly the model behaves in the two settings, and finds the vectors differ (cosine-similarity analysis, App. E.3). Braun covers the extraction-data aspect more directly.
3. "Its effect generalises unevenly \citep{tan2024generalisation}": **SUPPORTED**. Abstract: "while steering vectors often generalise well, for several concepts they are brittle to reasonable changes in the prompt".
